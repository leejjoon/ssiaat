Objective

Settled

What this is solving, and why it might actually be possible.

ssiaat can already get a SPHEREx bandpass transmission curve at any (band, central wavelength) — but only by loading the full spherex_tabular_bandpass package, which pulls in ~75 MB of parquet tables. Tabular_Bandpass_Lite already solved this exact problem for the central wavelength alone: six small hardcoded Chebyshev polynomials, zero dependency. The question this round of work is answering: can the same trick work for the full curve, by parameterizing it as sed.hflatline — a shape already used elsewhere in ssiaat for spectral line and continuum models — and fitting its shape parameters as smooth polynomials of wavelength, one set per band?

The user also asked a sharper question: a_left/a_right and dmu_left/dmu_right look like they might be correlated — could the parameter count be reduced before committing to six sets of polynomials? That's answered in the investigation below.

Investigation

4 findings

Measured directly against the real Tabular_Bandpass model, in the order they were found.

Finding 1 — the naive fit is degenerate, not noisy

Fitting hflatline per pixel with mu_center_left left free looked reasonable at first (RMS ~3–5×10⁻⁴), but the individual shape parameters swung by several percent between adjacent pixels. Measured cause: corr(dmu_left, a_left) = +1.000 within a 4-pixel window — a perfectly flat optimization ridge, not a physical trend. Raising the polynomial degree from 2 to 8 barely moved the residual, because there was no trend to fit.

Finding 2 — fixing the center costs nothing

The central wavelength at every pixel is already known exactly, from Tabular_Bandpass_Lite's own Chebyshev fit. Holding mu_center_left fixed at that value instead of re-deriving it removes the degeneracy — and measured fit quality is identical free vs. fixed:

Finding 3 — the parameter-reduction idea doesn't hold up

The hypothesis was that a_left≈a_right and dmu_left≈dmu_right closely enough to fit one shared value per pair instead of two. Measured per-band mean ratios (with mu_center fixed): a_left/a_right ranges 0.68–1.21 across the six bands; dmu_left/dmu_right ranges 0.75–1.02. Neither settles near a consistent value, and forcing symmetry costs up to 4.4× worse RMS in band 5 — a real accuracy loss for a ~30-float storage saving that doesn't justify it. The six bands keep independent dmu_left, a_left, dmu_right, a_right polynomials.

Finding 4 — normalization and the dichroic gap

The real bandpass is normalized to unit sum (discrete), not unit integral as the upstream docstring claims — so amplitude needs its own smooth per-band fit, not a shortcut via hflatline's own integral (which has no closed form for the asymmetric case anyway). Separately: bands 3 and 4 have a real gap near the 2.42 μm dichroic beam-splitter crossover, where the curve is chopped by the dichroic response and stops looking like an hflatline shape at all. Per this session's decision, the production module will extrapolate with a warning there rather than raising.

Part A — teach fit_hflatline a fixed center

Shipped

src/ssiaat/spherex_table.py — BandpassTool.fit_hflatline, fit_hflatline_at_iy (new)

Added a keyword-only mu_center parameter. Default None preserves the original free-fit behavior exactly — every pre-existing test still passes unchanged. Passing a float now holds mu_center_left fixed and drops it from the optimizer's free parameter vector, per Finding 2 above.

Also added fit_hflatline_at_iy(iy, ...), the row-indexed variant Part B's bulk sweep needs: get_bp_at_wvl inverts wavelength → row through an interpolator that rounds internally, so asking for a curve at a wavelength and separately asking for "the same row's" analytic center can silently land on different rows near template-quantization boundaries. fit_hflatline_at_iy instead calls the bandpass model directly on one integer row for both — no inversion, no mismatch.
